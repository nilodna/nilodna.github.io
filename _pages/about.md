I'm a Brazilian physical oceanographer, interested in how the ocean moves stuff around, from microparticles, such as plastic or organic carbon, to bigger things such as water masses. I apply a combination of numerical modeling, Lagrangian analysis and in situ measurements to understand accumulation hotspots, water masses' transformation and pathways, cross-shelf exchanges, and so on. Currently, I am investigating how seamounts affect the heat and mass transport along the Brazil Current's route, as well as how physical subduction is affected by these processes in driving the sinking of particulate organic carbon. I earned my degree in Oceanography (2016), and my Master's (2019) and PhD (2024) in Physical Oceanography from the University of São Paulo. I also spent time as a visiting student at the University of Massachusetts Dartmouth (2023) and a visiting researcher at Utrecht University (2025), broadening my research perspectives and collaborations.

I also have experience as a scientific consultant, where I applied my scientific programming skills at companies in the renewable energy sector and in operational forecasting for harbor applications. In this work, my responsibilities were focused on meteo-oceanographic data analysis, developing full ETL pipelines for ingesting satellite and reanalysis data, as well as for nowcasting precipitation, wind, currents, and sea level. My goal is to bridge rigorous science with practical applications, creating knowledge that informs both research and society.
